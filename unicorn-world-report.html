How it grew

Every entry below is a real request from the conversation, in the order it arrived. Nothing here was planned upfront — the world was built one instruction at a time, each one landing on top of whatever came before.

1

The starting world

A unicorn meadow for a 5-year-old: parrots circling overhead, a giraffe, and a cast of tap-to-react animals — no reading, no failing, no timers. Tapping the unicorn into ten falling stars sets off a confetti celebration.

feature
2

A horse that rears, a mermaid in the pond

The horse stands on its hind legs and paws the air on tap, hind legs planted; the mermaid sits on a rock at the pond's edge, tail trailing in the water, and sings when greeted.

feature
3

The first romance, and a monkey on a vine

Horse and unicorn fall for each other — hearts, a nuzzle, a soft chime — whenever they're close. A monkey swings from the big tree, chattering when tapped.

feature
4

Bug: a detached head

A screenshot caught the horse's head floating away from its neck mid-nuzzle — the head had been rotating around the wrong pivot. Fixed by making the neck and head one rigid group that pivots at the shoulder.

fix
5

Six more couples

Giraffe & monkey, bunny & sheep, fox & bear, frog & duck — each pair now sends hearts and triggers the other's reaction. The mermaid falls for every parrot that passes; the owl falls only for the pink one.

feature
6

Everyone learns to walk

The biggest rebuild: every creature — parrots and owl included — became independently walkable. Tap one to select it, tap the ground to send it there. Giraffes swing their legs, bunnies hop, snails crawl.

rebuild
7

A three-way romance

Butterfly, snail and turtle all love one another — tapping any one sends hearts to both others. At the turtle's walking speed, it's a slow-burn courtship.

feature
8

A nest, and a new score

Three speckled eggs appear under the owl's branch; tapping calls the pink parrot home to hatch them. Separately, the background loop was rebuilt from a plain sine-wave beep into a scheduled music-box waltz — plucked bell tones over a soft bass.

feature
9

A second nest, and dinner

The owl gets its own nest of two eggs beside the first. Once hatched, both parents now fly off for food — a berry, a ladybird — and feed their chicks on return, on request or on their own every so often.

feature
10

Day turns to night

A full day–night cycle: dusk fades the rainbow, stars come out, a smiling moon rises, fireflies drift over the grass, and the music softens. Every creature falls asleep except the owl. Each morning the nests' chicks grow — medium after the first night, full-grown after the second. Tapping the sun or moon skips ahead.

feature
11

Bug: sleeping wrong

The first pass at "lying down" just tipped each standing drawing onto its side — the horse ended up folded in half with its head through the pond. Replaced with a real pose per creature (folded legs, rested heads, a fox curled round its own tail) and sent the monkey home to curl up in the tree once night falls.

fix

Rough edges, honestly

Not everything landed clean the first time — two of the eleven requests above were fixes for things the previous pass got wrong. A few more things worth knowing:

  • Untested audio. All 22 sounds and the music-box waltz were written and syntax-checked, never heard — headless testing has no ears. The harmony should be sound; the mix balance between melody and bass is a guess.
  • Everything stays awake-capable at night. Sleeping animals can still be tapped and sent walking. A stricter version would make Sisi wait for morning — left as-is so nothing ever blocks her.
  • No save state. Refreshing the page resets the day/night clock, the chicks' growth stage, and who's paired up with whom. Everything else about the world (positions, love, nests) is recomputed fresh each load.
