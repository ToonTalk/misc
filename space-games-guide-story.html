First Day at the Game Studio

The app got a guide who never touches your work.

First Day at the Game Studio is a browser game where kids talk to an AI-shaped team, collect ideas, and turn them into a playable rocket rescue. It now has its own tour guide — a robot at the front desk who shows you around out loud, answers questions any time, and points at things instead of doing them for you.

What's new

A robot receptionist, not a walkthrough video

  • It narrates a tour. Press play and the guide walks you around the studio, lighting up the exact button it's talking about — the boss, the team, the workshop door — so you never have to guess where it means.

  • It answers questions, out loud or typed. Ask "where are the behavior cards?" any time, tour running or not, and it jumps straight there and points, instead of describing a screen you can't see.

  • It always knows what you've already done. It won't tell you to go accept a mission you already accepted, or send you looking for teammates you've already talked to.

  • It asks before it acts. If it offers to attach a card or answer a teammate for you, a small dialog checks first — "The guide wants to attach Horizontal Motion to the Astronaut. OK?" — so nothing happens to your game without your say-so.

How this was built

It took a lot more than one request

Adding the guide wasn't a single prompt that produced a finished feature. It was built, then actually played — clicked through like a curious kid would — and every session surfaced something the first version got wrong: a Reset button that silently did nothing because the browser was blocking its confirmation pop-up; a "studio is open" message that was true but scrolled off the bottom of the screen; the guide once quietly attaching a card the player hadn't actually asked for. Each of those became a real fix, not a patch — and after every single change, an automatic checklist re-ran to make sure nothing already working had quietly broken.

Once the guide worked well here, its underlying engine — the tour player, the spoken-question handler, the safety dialogs — was pulled out into a reusable piece, so the next app doesn't need its guide rebuilt from scratch. It's now being tried on a second, completely different app: a pixel-art drawing tool, to see how well the approach travels beyond the app it was designed for.
