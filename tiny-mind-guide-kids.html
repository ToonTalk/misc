This is a guide to read with a grown-up. Tiny Mind is an app where you raise a very small pretend mind. You feed it stories about a creature you invent, watch it learn, and then open it up like a scientist to find where it keeps what it learned.

YOUR HELPER

A friendly guide lives in a corner of the app. It says it is “a mind too — a much bigger one.” It talks you through every step, and it has a special mode for younger kids. If you ever feel lost, the guide is the thing to ask.

WHAT YOU DO

1.Invent a creature. Give it a name and three facts. Maybe Zog: a small dragon, who eats clouds, and lives behind the moon.
2.Make its storybook. Short stories about your creature, in easy words. You can write them, or ask the app to help write them with you.
3.Raise it. Press the button and watch the surprise line. At first the tiny mind is very surprised by your stories. As it learns, the surprise falls. When the line stops falling — it knows your creature as well as it ever will!
4.Ask it for a story. The best moment. Your creature, in a story the tiny mind made up itself.
5.Ride the time machine. Slide back through the tiny mind’s whole childhood and watch how its stories grew up.
6.Go hunting. Where does it keep your creature? Here is a secret before you start: a fact in a tiny mind is like a song in a choir — mute one singer and the song keeps going. No single brain-cell holds Zog. Lots of them share the job. The hunt shows you that, with real experiments you run yourself.
One honest thing. You learned about Zog from one story. The tiny mind needed fifty, read over and over and over. Minds like this do not learn the way you do — and you can see that for yourself in the app.

WHERE ITS STORIES CAME FROM

Before you ever met it, the tiny mind grew up on TinyStories — about two million very short stories written in the words a four-year-old knows. The famous big minds like Claude and ChatGPT are the same kind of thing, only millions of times bigger — far too big to open up and explore. That is exactly why the tiny one is special: it is small enough to look inside.

OPEN THE APP

Ask a grown-up to open the Tiny Mind app — everything you need loads by itself. Grown-ups: there is a guide for teachers and parents too.
