How your turtle guide works

Inside In-Between, a small turtle in a side panel can talk you through the app, answer questions out loud, and even press the buttons for you. Nothing about it is magic — here's exactly what happens, in plain English.

🐢 Turtle guide ready
Agoing beyond →

This is a working model of the panel, not the app itself — press it freely. It doesn't need a microphone, an internet connection, or an AI to run.

Five things happen, in order

What a question actually goes through

01
🎤

You ask

Press the microphone and talk, or type into the box at the bottom of the panel. Either way works the same from here on.

02
👂

It listens

Your browser turns your spoken words into text, right there on your own device. The guide only ever sees words — it never keeps a recording of your voice.

03
🤔

It thinks

The text of your question is sent to an AI language model — a computer program that has read enormous amounts of writing and learned to work out a helpful reply to almost anything you ask.

04
💬

It speaks

Your browser reads the answer aloud. The words also appear in the panel, so you can read along, or scroll back to anything it said earlier.

05
👉

Sometimes, it shows you

For some questions, describing the answer isn't enough. So the guide can actually press the app's own buttons and move its own sliders — the same ones you use — to demonstrate the answer while it talks.

Why won't the microphone turn on? If you're viewing In-Between inside another website or app, the microphone button may look pale and do nothing when pressed. That's not broken — that page simply isn't allowed to ask for microphone access. Type your question instead, or open the standalone version of In-Between directly in your own browser tab, where the microphone works normally.

Privacy, plainly

What leaves your device, and what doesn't

Every question you ask sits somewhere on a line between "stays on your computer" and "has to go out to get you an answer." Here's exactly where that line falls.

🏠 Stays with you

  • The sound of your voice
  • What you click, drag, or type while exploring the app
  • Everything the guide has said so far in the panel

→ Goes out, as text only

  • The words of your question
  • A short note on which part of the tour you're at, so the answer fits
  • Nothing else — no name, no files, no camera, ever

One more honest detail: turning speech into text uses your browser's own built-in tools, and in some browsers that briefly uses an online listening service to do the conversion — but no recording is kept afterwards, and it happens whether or not the guide is even switched on. It's a feature of the browser, not of In-Between.

Agency

You're always the one steering

The guide is built to give way the moment you want to drive:

⏸

Touch any control while the guide is mid-sentence, and it pauses itself — it notices you've taken over.

▶

Press play any time to pick up right where you paused.

❯

Hide the panel entirely with the arrow. A small turtle tab waits at the edge of the screen if you want it back.

👀

If you changed something while paused — a new shape, a different view — the guide notices and mentions it before carrying on, instead of pretending it didn't happen.

One last honesty

Where the answers actually come from

The "thinking" step isn't a search through a fixed list of pre-written answers, and it isn't a person typing back to you. It's a language model — predicting, word by word, what a good reply would look like, based on patterns it learned from a huge amount of text.

It's usually right about how In-Between works, because it's been told the details in advance. But like anyone explaining something from memory, it can occasionally get a small detail wrong. If an answer doesn't sound quite right, that's a good moment to ask again, or ask it a different way.
